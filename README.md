# Soft Object Deformation & Recovery Modeling

## 1. Test Subject & Physical Motivation

### 1.1 Object Selection
The primary test subject selected for this study is a standard **70 mm diameter open-cell polyurethane (PU) foam stress ball** ($m = 40.0\text{ g}$).

### 1.2 Engineering Justification
* **Spherical Boundary Symmetry:** A sphere provides an ideal geometric reference for contact mechanics. Initial contact occurs at a single theoretical point on top and bottom rigid boundaries, allowing direct observation of non-linear contact area expansion ($w_c \propto \sqrt{R_0 \cdot \Delta z}$).
* **Macroscopic Isotropy & Homogeneity:** Unlike fibrous, laminated, or 3D-printed soft structures, open-cell PU foam exhibits isotropic mechanical properties. The material responds identically regardless of load orientation, eliminating structural anisotropy as a confounding variable.
* **Dominance of Volumetric Collapse:** High porosity allows the investigation of volumetric strain ($\varepsilon_v \approx 57\text{–}86\%$) and cellular void collapse, distinguishing compressible cellular solids from incompressible elastomers (e.g., solid rubber, $\nu = 0.50$).
* **Repeatability & Bench-Top Accessibility:** The specimen maintains a stable elastic resting state over hundreds of deformation cycles without plastic deformation or mechanical hysteresis degradation.
